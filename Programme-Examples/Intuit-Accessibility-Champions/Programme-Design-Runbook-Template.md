# Accessibility Champions Programme Design Runbook Template

Use this template to design a programme that fits your organisation. It is a set of prompts and working fields, not a standard to implement unchanged. Complete it with the people who will sponsor, manage, participate in and be affected by the programme.

The Intuit example in this folder can prompt ideas about open entry, development levels, role-specific practice, leadership and community involvement. Adapt the design to your own context; do not assume Intuit’s activities, measures or thresholds are suitable for you.

## How to use this runbook

1. Start with a small design group that includes the accountable sponsor, programme lead, operational teams and people with relevant lived experience.
2. Mark each item as **confirmed**, **proposed**, **unknown** or **not applicable**. Do not turn an assumption into a commitment.
3. Agree the programme’s purpose, authority, capacity and intended outcomes before setting participation targets.
4. Pilot a manageable scope, gather accessible feedback and evidence, then revise the design.
5. Confirm responsibilities, resources and public claims with the people who have authority to do so.

### Questions

- Is participation open to all interested colleagues, targeted by role, or a combination?
- Are there accessible ways to join, learn, contribute asynchronously or pause participation?
- How will recruitment reach functions beyond digital product teams, such as customer support, HR, procurement, communications, operations and service delivery?
- How will you avoid relying on disabled colleagues to disclose or represent a whole group?
- How will the programme support different experience levels, locations, languages and working patterns?
- What manager support is required before a participant takes on tasks?

Do not use disability self-disclosure as an eligibility condition or programme metric. Collect only the personal information needed to operate the programme, with a stated purpose, access controls, retention period and appropriate privacy review.

### Activity areas to consider

Choose what fits the programme; do not treat this list as a mandatory curriculum.

- disability etiquette, inclusive communication and disability models;
- accessible design, content, procurement, engineering, testing or service operations;
- assistive technology demonstrations and supported practice;
- accessibility review of real work, with a named operational owner for findings;
- customer or employee research, conducted with accessible methods and appropriate consent;
- mentoring, peer learning, office hours and knowledge-sharing;
- accessible meetings, events, documents and communications;
- open-source contributions or professional learning, where relevant;
- customer issue investigation and follow-through;
- awareness, employee network and community activities.

For each activity, ask: **What will this change in the work? Who is responsible for acting on the learning or findings?**

## 7. Disability community and external partnerships

Plan reciprocal relationships rather than treating external communities as a source of free training or validation. Confirm current remit, access arrangements, safeguarding, costs and partnership terms directly with each organisation.

### Potential partner types to explore

- disabled people’s organisations and disability-led groups;
- local or national charities and not-for-profit organisations;
- disability employee networks and cross-organisation champions communities, including the [Champions of Accessibility Network (CAN)](../../CAN_Connecting_Accessibility_Champions_Networks.md);
- condition-specific, sensory, mobility, learning disability and neurodivergence communities where relevant to the programme’s scope;
- universities, accessibility research groups and inclusive design communities;
- relevant open-source, professional and industry networks;
- community programmes led by people with lived experience.

### Questions

- Which communities are relevant to the actual people and services in scope?

- How will participants correct or delete information?
- Will dashboards show only appropriate aggregate information and include context?
- Are screenshots, research notes, customer cases or submitted portfolios free of confidential and personal data?

## 10. Measures and evaluation

Define a baseline and intended outcomes before setting targets. Separate programme activity, network health, organisational practice and human outcomes.

| Evidence area | What to learn | Possible evidence | Limits / interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Reach and access | Who can participate and who faces barriers? | Participation by role or location where appropriate; access feedback | Participation counts do not show influence or outcomes |
| Network health | Do members feel supported, connected and able to contribute? | Accessible pulse feedback, retention, peer support | Avoid treating lower attendance as lack of commitment without context |
| Practice change | Are accessibility activities entering normal work? | Workflow changes, accountable actions, early issue resolution | Attribute contribution proportionately; consider other causes |
| Service or workplace experience | Did people experience improved access or fewer barriers? | Accessible research, service evidence, issue follow-up | Evidence needs scope, context and participation from affected people |
| Programme sustainability | Is the programme resourced and adapting? | Protected time, manager support, specialist capacity, review actions | Approved resources and actual capacity may differ |

### Questions

- What evidence would change a decision or improve the programme?
- Whose experience is missing from the evidence?
- How will feedback be made accessible and safe?
- Which results will be reported, to whom, and with what limitations?
- How will you distinguish correlation, contribution and causation?
- What findings will trigger a change in activities, resources or scope?
