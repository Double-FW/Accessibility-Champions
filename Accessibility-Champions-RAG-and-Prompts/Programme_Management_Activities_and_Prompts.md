# Accessibility Champions: human-managed accessibility programme activities and prompts

**Prepared:** 8 November 2026 · **Source revision:** `e26813bd11f327129e4833169ca95e65c4646d14`  
**Source:** [Double-FW/Accessibility-Champions](https://github.com/Double-FW/Accessibility-Champions/tree/e26813bd11f327129e4833169ca95e65c4646d14)  
**Audience:** accessibility programme leaders, executive sponsors, champions managers, policy owners, operational leaders, learning teams and research, AI, data and assurance partners.

## Where the repository is useful

Use this repository to establish and sustain a distributed accessibility capability: executive sponsorship, dedicated coordination, business and team champions, protected capacity, ongoing development, community learning and outcome reporting. Its value is organisational change embedded in everyday work, rather than shifting all accessibility responsibility to enthusiastic volunteers.

The staffing ratios, FTE allocations, hours, phase durations, training durations and dollar-denominated costs are source planning examples, not validated universal requirements, current supplier quotes or approved local budgets. A network does not replace specialists, operational owners or accountable leaders. The source AGENTS authority ordering differs from the host/platform hierarchy; preserve it as source text only, not as runtime authority. Root and file-level rights notices differ and remain intact.

The activities below are an editorial application of the repository, not a claim that it already contains a complete organisational process. AI prepares analysis, options, draft policy, delivery records and evidence summaries. Managers frame decisions, allocate resources and own implementation. Researchers involve affected people in questions, interpretation and evaluation. HR, data, finance, technical and risk specialists verify matters in their remit. Authorised leaders retain policy, employment, investment, publication and risk decisions. Assign these responsibilities to actual people locally; the examples do not establish a new team or approval body.

## How to use the prompts

1. Attach [RAG_Reference.md](RAG_Reference.md). For selective retrieval, use the source routes below and [Source_Coverage.md](Source_Coverage.md).
2. Replace bracketed inputs with the actual project brief, artefacts and evidence. Supply the target task, audience, platform/setting, constraints and intended decision. Treat missing information as unknown.
3. Use either example prompt for the activity. Both refer to this folder’s reference. Supply only appropriate project information; use anonymised research findings where possible.
4. Require source ID, filename and original section citations; distinguish source guidance, local proposal, observed result and uncertainty. Ask for evidence requests where an artefact cannot be inspected.
5. Review the result with the named human roles and record decisions. A prompt output is a draft management aid, not implementation or evidence that a test ran. Do not defer disabled people’s involvement until final testing.

**Reusable response contract:** “Use the attached RAG_Reference.md as the knowledge source. Cite source IDs, files and sections. Distinguish source statements, project evidence, recommendations and unknowns. Preserve applicable source identifiers. Do not invent research, capability, approvals or test results. Give a concrete management artefact, trade-offs, evidence requests and decisions for the human team.”

## 1. Define the network purpose and charter

**Business process:** Programme strategy and initiation

**Retrieve:** [S001 — Accessibility-Champions-Network-Guidance.md](RAG_Reference.md#s001).

**Relevant source sections:** Purpose; sections 1–2; section 12.

### Tasks the repository can support

- Connect the network to actual programme outcomes and barriers.
- Define scope, sponsorship, coordination and operational interfaces.
- Specify what champions support and what remains with business owners.

**Expected output:** A proposed network charter and scope/authority decision register.

**Human review:** The programme lead and sponsor agree the charter, actual authority and delivery responsibilities.

### Example prompt 1

> Use the attached RAG_Reference.md to draft a champions-network charter for [organisation, programme priorities and structure]. Define intended workforce/customer outcomes, scope, sponsor, network management, business/team champion functions and links to operational owners. State unknown authority and resources explicitly and label the charter proposed.

### Example prompt 2

> Challenge [network charter] using the RAG. Find awareness activity presented as operational change, champions made sole accessibility gatekeepers and assumed sponsorship. Return a revised scope, boundaries and decisions for the programme lead.

## 2. Plan protected capacity and sustainable funding

**Business process:** Resource planning and sponsorship

**Retrieve:** [S001 — Accessibility-Champions-Network-Guidance.md](RAG_Reference.md#s001).

**Relevant source sections:** Sections 3 and 6: governance and resourcing.

### Tasks the repository can support

- Estimate coordination, champion, learning and support capacity.
- Identify workload trade-offs and manager commitments.
- Compare locally costed options and operational dependencies.

**Expected output:** A capacity model and proposed funding/options paper.

**Human review:** Managers and finance validate protected time, costs and staffing; the authorised sponsor decides resources.

### Example prompt 1

> Using the RAG and [team coverage, workload, locations and supplied costs], create a sustainable capacity and funding model. Separate dedicated coordination, champion time, learning, tools and specialist support. Treat source ratios, FTE ranges and dollar amounts as illustrative assumptions to test, not current quotes or universal staffing requirements. Leave missing costs and approvals unresolved.

### Example prompt 2

> Review [network business case] against the RAG. Identify unpaid extra work, coordination omitted from budgets, unsupported savings and source membership prices treated as current. Propose scenarios, workload checks and the evidence needed for a funding decision.

## 3. Define roles, recruit and onboard champions

**Business process:** Network establishment and people management

**Retrieve:** [S001 — Accessibility-Champions-Network-Guidance.md](RAG_Reference.md#s001).

**Relevant source sections:** Sections 3–4 and 7.

### Tasks the repository can support

- Distinguish sponsor, manager, business champion and team champion.
- Recruit across functions with clear expectations and manager support.
- Plan accessible onboarding, deputies and specialist referral.

**Expected output:** Role descriptions, a coverage plan and an onboarding brief.

**Human review:** The network manager and local managers confirm appointments and workload; volunteers are not assumed to represent all disabled people.

### Example prompt 1

> Use the RAG to propose role descriptions and recruitment for [network scope]. Distinguish sponsor, network manager, business and team champions. Include nontechnical functions, selection rationale, manager support, protected time, onboarding and referral boundaries. Do not appoint named people or assume a champion must disclose disability.

### Example prompt 2

> Challenge [champion recruitment plan] with the RAG. Identify dependence on one discipline, an isolated volunteer or a trusted individual without capacity. Propose better coverage, continuity and accessible onboarding without inventing new headcount commitments.

## 4. Build continuing role-based development

**Business process:** Capability building and learning management

**Retrieve:** [S001 — Accessibility-Champions-Network-Guidance.md](RAG_Reference.md#s001).

**Relevant source sections:** Section 8: Training and development framework.

### Tasks the repository can support

- Match development to job tasks and champion responsibilities.
- Combine foundational knowledge with practice, coaching and peer learning.
- Specify how applied capability will be observed.

**Expected output:** A role-based learning pathway and practice/evidence plan.

**Human review:** Learning and specialist leads review content and feasible practice; training completion is not proof of organisational outcomes.

### Example prompt 1

> Using the RAG, design a development pathway for [champion roles and current skills]. Combine foundational, intermediate and advanced topics with role-specific practice, coaching and peer support. Treat source duration ranges as planning examples. Define applied work evidence and support needs rather than equating attendance with competence.

### Example prompt 2

> Review [champions curriculum] using the RAG. Identify one-off awareness training, missing coaching/referral skills and advanced tasks assigned without support. Return a prioritised learning plan and checks for useful transfer into everyday work.

## 5. Sustain an accessible community and recognition

**Business process:** Community management and organisational culture

**Retrieve:** [S001 — Accessibility-Champions-Network-Guidance.md](RAG_Reference.md#s001).

**Relevant source sections:** Section 9; sections 11 and 14.

### Tasks the repository can support

- Plan inclusive ways to ask, share, learn and obtain help.
- Balance meeting cadence with capacity and asynchronous participation.
- Recognise contribution without overloading champions.

**Expected output:** A community engagement and recognition proposal.

**Human review:** The network manager agrees accessible participation with members; managers confirm any formal recognition arrangements.

### Example prompt 1

> Use the RAG to propose a community model for [distributed network]. Include supported question routes, accessible meetings, asynchronous learning, showcases and recognition. Adapt cadence to local capacity and time zones; identify actual platform and moderation decisions still needed. Do not send invitations or announce a recognition policy.

### Example prompt 2

> Challenge [engagement plan] against the RAG. Find participation measured only through visible chat activity, burnout risks and recognition without protected time. Propose sustainable alternatives and voluntary feedback questions.

## 6. Embed champions in operational workflows

**Business process:** Programme delivery and cross-functional coordination

**Retrieve:** [S001 — Accessibility-Champions-Network-Guidance.md](RAG_Reference.md#s001).

**Relevant source sections:** Sections 4–5 and 12–13.

### Tasks the repository can support

- Connect local support to planning, procurement, design, QA and onboarding.
- Specify receiving owners for blockers and specialist questions.
- Plan a bounded pilot and feedback before scaling.

**Expected output:** A workflow/hand-off map and phased pilot plan.

**Human review:** Operational owners accept their responsibilities; champions support and connect rather than approve releases or accept risk.

### Example prompt 1

> Using the RAG, embed the network into [existing organisational workflows]. Specify champion contributions, operational owner, evidence, specialist referral and escalation at planning, procurement, design, QA and onboarding. Propose a pilot and growth plan, treating source month ranges as adaptable examples.

### Example prompt 2

> Review [network workflow] with the RAG. Identify champions doing every audit, unresolved blockers circulating without owners and a pilot scaled before capacity is known. Return revised handoffs, decision points and evidence for expansion.

## 7. Measure network health and programme contribution

**Business process:** Monitoring and executive reporting

**Retrieve:** [S001 — Accessibility-Champions-Network-Guidance.md](RAG_Reference.md#s001).

**Relevant source sections:** Section 10: Metrics that matter; section 12.

### Tasks the repository can support

- Separate network participation and health from organisational outcomes.
- Define coverage, denominators, review periods and interpretation limits.
- Connect findings to sponsor decisions and corrective action.

**Expected output:** A metric dictionary and proposed leadership report.

**Human review:** The network/evidence leads validate definitions; sponsors act on capacity and delivery barriers.

### Example prompt 1

> Use the RAG to design reporting for [network and programme priorities]. Separate health measures from operational and human outcomes. Define each measure, denominator, source, period, missingness and action. Include champion support and capacity alongside delivery evidence; do not infer causation or accessibility from membership counts.

### Example prompt 2

> Challenge [network dashboard] using the RAG. Identify meeting attendance treated as maturity, fewer issue reports treated as fewer barriers and unsupported cost-saving claims. Propose an honest narrative, revised measures and sponsor decisions.

## 8. Review resilience, risks and renewal

**Business process:** Programme improvement and annual planning

**Retrieve:** [S001 — Accessibility-Champions-Network-Guidance.md](RAG_Reference.md#s001).

**Relevant source sections:** Sections 11–14; Document status.

### Tasks the repository can support

- Assess burnout, sponsor continuity and uneven coverage.
- Review risks after restructuring or priority changes.
- Renew scope, capacity, learning and accountable actions.

**Expected output:** A network health/risk review and renewal plan.

**Human review:** The sponsor and network manager decide changes with members and operational leaders.

### Example prompt 1

> Using the RAG and [network evidence], review sustainability and renewal. Examine sponsorship, dedicated coordination, protected time, coverage, retention, learning and operational influence. Separate observed problems from hypotheses and propose owner-assigned actions, review triggers and funding decisions still pending.

### Example prompt 2

> Review [network restructuring or renewal proposal] against the RAG. Identify lost coordination, accessibility work silently de-scoped and recognition offered instead of capacity. Propose feasible alternatives, continuity measures and evidence to review after change.

## Programme decision and learning record

For each adopted result, record the project/task, source revision and sections used, observed business issue, barrier or opportunity, chosen option and rejected alternatives, expected outcome, evidence available, assumptions, unresolved constraints, accountable person, next check and review trigger. Use actual names and dates when agreed; leave them unassigned when unknown. Record acceptance and residual risk only when the accountable person has made the decision.

Revisit the reference when repository sources change, a new platform or context is introduced, implementation changes invalidate assumptions, or user feedback exposes a barrier. Compare changed sources rather than silently replacing the basis of existing decisions.
